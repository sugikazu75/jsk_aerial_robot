# gimbalrotor

[日本語](README.ja.md)

Multirotors whose rotors are tilted by gimbals. The airframes are in `robots/<airframe>/`; this page
covers `quad_with_dual_arm` (four gimbal rotors, two 3-DoF pitch arms, a hook module on the front arm)
and its hanging-perching motion.

## Bringup

```bash
# real machine
roslaunch gimbalrotor bringup.launch airframe:=quad_with_dual_arm
# simulation (Gazebo)
roslaunch gimbalrotor bringup.launch airframe:=quad_with_dual_arm real_machine:=false simulation:=true headless:=false
# simulation (MuJoCo), with the bar and the wall of the perching task
roslaunch gimbalrotor bringup.launch airframe:=quad_with_dual_arm real_machine:=false simulation:=true headless:=false \
  mujoco:=true mujoco_world:=quad_with_dual_arm_perching
# joystick (on /gimbalrotor/joy)
roslaunch aerial_robot_base joy_stick.launch robot_name:=gimbalrotor
```

The MuJoCo model (`mujoco/quad_with_dual_arm/`) is generated by `catkin build` from
`config/mujoco_model.yaml`. The world `worlds/quad_with_dual_arm_perching.xml` includes it; its initial
state is in `worlds/quad_with_dual_arm_perching.yaml`.

## Perching: plan, optimise, run

| step | command | parameters | output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. plan | `rosrun gimbalrotor plan_perching_motion.py` | `config/quad_with_dual_arm/perching_motion.yaml` | `config/quad_with_dual_arm/trajectories/perching_motion.json` (+ `.png`) |
| 2. optimise | `rosrun aerial_to_solver optimize_trajectory.py <1.json>` | `aerial_to_solver/config/optimize_trajectory.yaml` | `perching_motion_optimized.json` (+ `.png`) |
| 3. view | `roslaunch gimbalrotor perching_motion.launch trajectory_file:=<json>` | launch arguments | RViz playback |
| 4. run | `rosrun gimbalrotor perching_task.py __ns:=gimbalrotor [_takeoff:=true]` | `config/quad_with_dual_arm/follow_trajectory.yaml`, `perching_task.yaml` | the robot (simulation or real) |

Every parameter is in its YAML file, and each one can be overridden: `--key value` for steps 1 and 2,
`_key:=value` for step 4. Step 2 is described in `aerial_to_solver/README.md`.

### Scripts

`scripts/` is grouped into `planning/` (trajectory planning), `execution/` (trajectory and task
execution), `visualization/` (RViz), and `simulation/` (MuJoCo model and world generation).
The `rosrun gimbalrotor <script>.py` commands are unchanged.

**`plan_perching_motion.py`** plans a collision-checked flight to the bar, constructs the hook,
hang, reach and push phases with IK, and solves static thrust and contact forces for each frame.
It writes a JSON trajectory and, unless `--no-plot` is set, a plot. It runs offline without a ROS
master. `perching_motion.yaml` sets the scene, constraints, timing and output path; command-line
options can override individual values. Collision and statics failures are reported in the output
summary, while the trajectory is still written for inspection.

**`trajectory_playback.py`** reads a planned or optimised JSON trajectory and publishes its root
TF, joint states, rotor thrust, contact wrenches, coloured path and contact phase for RViz. It does
not command the robot. `perching_motion.launch` starts it with the robot model and RViz; `~speed`,
`~loop` and `~pause` control playback.

**`follow_trajectory.py`** sends the file's CoG, body attitude and arm joint targets to the robot.
It can arm and take off, move to the first frame, settle, then play through `~end_frame` and hold
there. It does not judge contact or switch controller contacts. `follow_trajectory.yaml` sets the
file, playback speed, target rate and initial move; thrust and gimbal values in the file are not sent.

**`perching_task.py`** uses the same trajectory follower but adds the perching sequence: flight,
convergence before the hook approach, a slower approach, operator OK/NG, contact switching and the
remaining trajectory. After NG, it releases target control for teleoperation and can return to the
approach start for another attempt. `perching_task.yaml` sets these decisions; `after_hook: stop`
ends at the hooked pose for a subsequent task.

**`hanging_teleop.py`** takes a rear-hand target from an RViz interactive marker or
`~target` (`PoseStamped`). Its whole-body IK moves the root, body pitch and arms while keeping the
front hook on the bar and respecting joint, torque and collision constraints. It sends CoG,
attitude and arm targets at `~rate`; `hanging_teleop.yaml` sets speeds, limits and IK weights.

**`contact_wrench_visualizer.py`** subscribes to each contact force published by the whole-body
controller, publishes contact-frame TF from the controller's contact parameters, and republishes
the forces on `~contact` for RViz. It only visualizes controller output; `bringup.launch` starts it.

### 1. Plan (`perching_motion.yaml`)

The command line script defines the motion phases and file output. Perching frames, hook IK tasks,
and contact policy are in `src/gimbalrotor/perching_model.py`; the task parameters remain in
`config/quad_with_dual_arm/perching_motion.yaml`. Reusable IK, free-flyer kinematics, collision
scene management, RRTConnect search, trajectory interpolation and basic statics helpers are in the
separate `aerial_motion_planning` package. Rotor allocation and its constraints are in
`src/gimbalrotor/rotor_statics.py`.

The motion: hover → flight (RRTConnect) → stop in front of the bar → approach along the opening of the
hook → load the bar → hang → reach the wall with the rear arm → push. Main groups of keys:

- `bar_*`, `wall_x`: the scene. Use the same values in `perching_motion.launch` and the MuJoCo world.
- `start`, `hook_tilt`, `push_force`, `approach`: the task.
- `min_lift`, `max_effort_ratio`, `max_thrust_tilt`: the statics (thrust kept up, torque margin).
- `reference_attitude`, `max_body_tilt`, `attitude_mask`, `attitude_weight`: the body attitude in the IK and the flight. Set an `attitude_mask` component to 0 to hold that axis at its reference.
- `margin`, `bar_margin`: clearances in flight (`bar_margin` for the front arm passing over the bar).
- `*_time`, `flight_*speed`: the timing (frames = duration × `rate`).

### 4. Run (`follow_trajectory.yaml`, `perching_task.yaml`)

#### Stage 1: deformation flight before the hook approach

```bash
rosrun gimbalrotor check_pre_hook_motion.py __ns:=gimbalrotor _takeoff:=true
```

This runs the existing SMACH through `TAKEOFF → GO_TO_START → FLIGHT → PRE_HOOK_CHECK`. It stops
`approach_window` seconds before the first contact (about 11.4 s, frame 228 in the current plan), checks
that the CoG position and velocity and arm joints have converged, then exits. It does not play the
hook approach or activate a contact. Navigation holds the last target afterward; land with the usual
flight controls. Use `_takeoff:=false` if already hovering. It defaults to the newly planned
`perching_motion.json`; override with `_trajectory_file:=...`. Convergence settings come from
`perching_task.yaml`.

`perching_task.py` is a SMACH state machine (watch it with `rosrun smach_viewer smach_viewer.py`):

```
TAKEOFF → GO_TO_START → FLIGHT → HOOK → JUDGE_HOOK ─OK→ AFTER_HOOK → succeeded
                                  ↑         │NG
                                RETURN ←OK─ TELEOP ─NG→ finished
```

- HOOK waits in front of the bar until the CoG and the joints converge (`converge_*`), then plays the
  approach at `approach_speed_scale`. Not converged in `converge_timeout`: TELEOP.
- JUDGE_HOOK and TELEOP wait for the operator: OK = R1 (`ok_button`), NG = R2 (`ng_button`), or
  ```bash
  rostopic pub -1 /gimbalrotor/perching_task/ok std_msgs/Empty
  rostopic pub -1 /gimbalrotor/perching_task/ng std_msgs/Empty
  ```
- TELEOP sends no targets, so the robot flies with the sticks; OK returns to the start of the
  approach in a straight line (clear the bar first) and retries.

`follow_trajectory.py` only plays the requested frames (`_end_frame:=267` to stop before the first contact in the current sample trajectory,
using the frame number found during trajectory review). It has no contact decisions. The perching
approach, operator checks, and contact switching belong to `perching_task.py`, with its own
`perching_task.yaml` (`_task_config:=...` to replace it). Both send the CoG and the attitude on `uav/nav` and the arm joints on
`arm_joints_ctrl`; the thrust and the gimbals of the file are not used.

### Whole-body controller (contacts)

`bringup.launch whole_body:=true` replaces the allocation of the gimbalrotor controller: the wrench of
the CoG and attitude PIDs is shared between the rotors and the active contacts by a QP of the
whole-body statics (friction cones, joint torque limits, a least lift per rotor); while a contact
carries load, the PIDs hold their integral. Parameters: `config/quad_with_dual_arm/GimbalrotorWholeBodyControl(_sim).yaml`
(`controller/whole_body`, with the contacts). A contact is switched by

```bash
rosservice call /gimbalrotor/whole_body/contacts/hook_bar/activate true
```

and its load moves on over its `ramp_time`. `perching_task.py` does it (`switch_contacts`): the hook when
the operator confirms it, the other contacts where they begin and end in the trajectory file.

## Hanging: moving the rear hand (whole-body IK)

Both the planner and the hanging controller use `aerial_motion_planning.ik.IKProblem` for coordinate bounds and masks, optional contact equalities, and optional collision checks. The planner uses bounded nonlinear least squares; hanging uses incremental ProxQP solves so that its contact can be a hard equality. The point, direction, CoM and torque tasks remain defined by each caller.

While the robot hangs on the bar, `hanging_teleop.py` moves the rear hand wherever the operator puts
it. A whole-body IK runs in the plane of the arms (`src/gimbalrotor/hanging_ik.py`). Its variables
are the root (along the heading and in height), the body pitch and the six arm joints. It holds the
hook point on the bar as an equality, keeps the angle of the hook and the CoM below the hook, and
stays within the joint limits, the body pitch (`max_pitch`), the static torques of the rear arm
(`max_effort_ratio`) and self-collisions. A target out of reach stops the hand at the closest point,
without pulling the hook off the bar. The result goes to the CoG, the attitude and the arm joints
every cycle.

```bash
# hanging on the bar (e.g. after the hooking of perching_task.py, with an end_frame in the hang)
rosrun gimbalrotor hanging_teleop.py __ns:=gimbalrotor
# the target of the hand: in RViz, an InteractiveMarkers display on
#   /gimbalrotor/hanging_teleop/target_marker/update (drag the arrow; turn the ring for the direction)
# or a pose (the direction of the hand is the x axis of the orientation; projected onto the plane)
rostopic pub -1 /gimbalrotor/hanging_teleop/target geometry_msgs/PoseStamped \
  '{header: {frame_id: world}, pose: {position: {x: -0.4, y: 0.0, z: 1.0}, orientation: {y: 1.0, w: 0.0}}}'
```

The hook point, the plane and the starting posture are those of the robot when the node starts; the
hook contact of the whole-body controller is switched on (`activate_hook`). The parameters (speeds,
limits, IK weights) are in `config/quad_with_dual_arm/hanging_teleop.yaml`; `~markers` shows the hook
point, the hand and the CoM of the IK.
